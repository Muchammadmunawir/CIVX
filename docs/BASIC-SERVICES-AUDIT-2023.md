# CIVX Basic Services audit — Jawa Barat 2023

**Audit access date:** 28 October 2026. **Geography:** all 27 Jawa Barat kabupaten/kota, using distinct canonical BPS identifiers for 18 kabupaten and nine kota. This is a **research and next-methodology admission decision**, not a scoring cutover.

**Recommendation:** keep the three current indicators, with Puskesmas explicitly labelled a primary-care **supply proxy**. Add two next-model candidates: the published household crowding share **≤7.2 m²/person**, and **any health-insurance ownership**, derived from the explicit no-insurance share. Use three subdimensions, not five flat independently weighted inputs. No weights are assigned.

No frontend, UI, production scorer, weights, methodology configuration, recommendation engine, production observations or current Civic Index changed. The earlier Socioeconomic/Education audit and existing worktree were retained.

## Recovery and evidence boundary

### Admission rules

CORE requires: (1) official source; (2) 2023 reference year; (3) 27/27 numeric district coverage; (4) comparable definition; (5) comparable unit; (6) compatible administrative geography; (7) defensible direction for the **literal measured construct**; (8) no unresolved missing values; (9) adequate regional variation; and (10) no severe unnecessary redundancy. Availability alone is not admission. Current datasets are not assumed to measure travel access, safety, affordability or reliability they do not observe.

The five recommended indicators pass these gates under their stated definitions. Their ten pairwise correlations are all below |r|=0.85. This does not establish causal independence, statistically significant ranks, final weights or a validated aggregate model. Official estimates remain estimates; no missing region was estimated, zero-filled, interpolated or replaced by another year.

### Raw versus derived and the actual current formula

Sanitation and water are **published rates**, already percentage-based but not CIVX-normalized. Only localized decimal parsing and canonical district matching are required. Do not calculate them from unrelated resident totals.

Puskesmas is genuinely **derived**. Actual code in `scripts/build-civic-current.mjs:67–83` validates both periods and units, then computes:

`Puskesmas availability = main Puskesmas count / exact 2023 resident population × 100000`

The code preserves numerator, denominator, formula and both source references. All 27 published production values exactly equal this formula; the builder was not run and no production release was regenerated.

- **Numerator:** yearbook 4.2.7, total 2023 column (11), printed 236 / PDF 312: **1,106** main Puskesmas. Inpatient 280 + non-inpatient 826. Exclude Puskesmas Pembantu, mobile units, clinics, Posyandu and hospitals. Explicit totals are used; a dash means negligible under this book, not an automatic zero.
- **Denominator:** health-profile appendix 1 / PDF 180, column (7), **49,860,330** residents across the same 27 regions. Fresh original PDF acquisition succeeded and matched its archived SHA-256. Every district matches the current denominator. BPS population-projection books independently corroborate every value after rounding to 0.01 thousand, but do not supply exact persons. They are not silently substituted.
- **Vintage caution:** health-profile age/visit tables instead use 49,899,992. Its water table 88 uses still other populations, e.g. Bogor 3,826,475 instead of 5,627,021. None replaces the chosen common denominator. Projection vintage is corroborated by all 27 matches, not an explicit within-year date.
- **Meaning:** facility location per resident describes supply intensity. Rural distance needs, private alternatives, catchments, staffing, opening hours and cross-border care remain unmeasured. Do not call this observed healthcare access.

### Decisions that matter beyond availability

- **Housing:** the adequate-housing composite is genuinely measurable for 27/27 regions, with matching district RSE 1.80–10.18%. It jointly includes structure, living space, improved water and sanitation. It is an **alternative bundle**, not an extra score beside its own components. Select the standalone crowding share instead. Preserve the published **≤7.2 m²** band: the composite source says sufficiency **≥7.2 m²**, so equality appears in both statements. Do not silently relabel the crowding band as the exact `<7.2` complement. Roof/wall/floor marginals cannot reconstruct household intersections or affordability.
- **Formal financial access:** `100 − percentage reporting no health insurance` gives any ownership without adding overlapping insurer categories. It is not administrative active-JKN coverage, verified benefits, claim success or needed care received. Its direction is lower **formal insurance exclusion**, not a claim of comprehensive realized access. It adds a different service barrier from facility supply. The source provides no-insurance SE/RSE; the complement has the same SE in percentage points.
- **Electricity:** actual household electrical-main-lighting share combines PLN and non-PLN. It is not a separate administrative electrification ratio, raw household count, grid reliability or connection capacity. PLN-only excludes served non-PLN users. Saturation blocks CORE usefulness for this cohort, not local attention to remaining gaps.
- **Digital:** no verified 27-region 2023 **household internet-access or fixed-broadband** panel was acquired. The national household table supplies only Jawa Barat 87.92%, not 27 district observations. The complete district substitute measures **persons aged 5+ using the internet in the preceding three months**. Keep it as digital-participation context, not relabelled household availability. Its relationship with expenditure is not by itself grounds for exclusion; the construct mismatch is decisive for this access-focused model.
- **Waste:** a 27-region published administrative service percentage exists. That does not establish its denominator, household service universe or safe management. Handled-versus-managed labels conflict; handled mass exceeds matched generation for Majalengka, Pangandaran and Kota Tasikmalaya. No manufactured corrected rate is admitted.
- **Water quality:** improved, piped, safe-labelled, clean-source, inspected-facility and PDAM-customer concepts differ. A piped source is not necessarily safer than every protected non-piped alternative. BPS safe-labelled panels are complete, but captured metadata does not establish the full operational algorithm or laboratory compliance. SDS K00038 describes physical-quality conditions; K00026 separately includes biological/chemical conditions. Do not infer the exact live-table algorithm from a generic concept name.
- **Healthcare capacity:** counts and derived rates are real, but all-provider staff capture, possible duplicated reports, FTE, referral catchments and snapshot differences remain unresolved. The source itself warns about reporting limits. Main Puskesmas remains one transparent first-level supply measure; hospitals, beds and several staff series do not receive independent CORE slots merely because available.

## E. Statistical quality report

### Method and complete coverage

Descriptives use published precision, canonical district joins and unweighted regional means. These means are **not provincial weighted rates**. SD is population SD over observed regions (`sqrt(sum((x−mean)^2)/count)`), with count 27 for complete panels. Partial-series summaries remain labelled incomplete. CV is 100×SD/mean for positive means; small shares and bounded ceilings require substantive interpretation. No observation is clipped, winsorized or deleted.

Quartiles use type 7 interpolation. Outside 1.5 IQR is a screening flag, including points outside 3 IQR; it is not a source-error verdict. Pearson uses untransformed values; Spearman uses average ranks. The declared 43 complete candidate/alternative panels produce 903 full 27-region pairs, with 55 possible-redundancy flags at `|r|≥0.85`. Raw size counts, denominator series, republications and nominal material/device/sex components are not put into the overall correlation network. They still receive descriptives below.

## G. Explicit answers to the ten final questions

1. **Are the current three sufficient?** No, not for a broad Basic Services construct. They cover WASH and one primary-care supply input, not housing crowding, financial coverage, digital connectivity, energy reliability or waste services. Their limited scope is preferable to invented completeness.
2. **Which current indicators remain?** Keep all three. Narrow Puskesmas wording to supply capacity. Do not remove or alter any production indicator in this task.
3. **Which additions have strongest evidence?** The literal household crowding ≤7.2 m²/person share and any health-insurance ownership, `100−none`. Both have 27/27 official 2023 records, useful variation, clear directions for their declared constructs and no severe overlap with selected components. Ownership is not verified active coverage.
4. **Which remain context only?** Saturated electrical lighting; personal internet usage, age 5+, and computer ownership; adequate-housing composite as an alternative bundle; hospitals/beds/doctor/nurse/midwife capacity alternatives; piped, safe-labelled and clean-source water variants; water monitoring; administrative waste-service percentage/tonnage; cooking-energy mix. Rejected interpretations remain explicitly separated in B/C.
5. **Is healthcare overrepresented?** It would be if Puskesmas, hospitals, doctors, beds and staff variants each received independent weight. Several r exceed 0.85 and the same institutions underpin the counts. Select one primary-care supply proxy plus a genuinely different formal financial-access barrier within one healthcare subdimension. Its eventual budget is not determined here.
6. **Is electricity too saturated?** Yes as this cohort's general CORE rank discriminator: 99.76–100.00%, 21 rounded 100 values, SD 0.055239 points. Keep remaining deprivation context; this does not establish universal reliable power.
7. **Is internet access CORE-ready?** Not as requested household service availability. No full 27-region household/broadband access panel was verified. Comparable personal usage, age 5+, exists, but measures behavior/participation shaped by connectivity, resources, age and skills. Keep context without relabelling it household coverage. Correlation alone is not the rejection rule.
8. **Is adequate housing measurable consistently?** Yes: 27 published composite values and 27 matching precision records exist. It is not an independent add-on beside its own water/sanitation/crowding criteria. Choose standalone crowding for this hierarchy; a composite replacement would need an explicit alternative-method decision.
9. **Is waste coverage CORE-ready?** No. The complete published service percentage fails denominator/scope reconciliation; some mass relationships are impossible under the proposed interpretation. The recovered SIPSN investigation did not acquire 2023 historical official records. That is an evidence-access gap, not proof districts reported zero or the data never existed. Solid waste is conceptually distinct from excreta sanitation, so valid future evidence could add a distinct service.
10. **What final structure is strongest?** Section D's three subdimensions and five precise indicators. Keep Utilities, Digital Participation and Waste Services as unscored evidence profiles until their own admission gaps close. Do not force empty scored domains or assign final weights.
